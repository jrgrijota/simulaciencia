## What you will learn

With the balancing equations simulation you will see that in a chemical reaction atoms neither appear nor disappear: they are only rearranged. You will learn to balance chemical equations, you will check Lavoisier's and Proust's laws and you will find out what the limiting reactant is.

**How to work.** Each activity has three steps:

1. **Predict.** Before looking at the simulation, write down what you think will happen and why.
2. **Observe.** Set up the simulation, watch what happens and note down the data.
3. **Explain.** Compare what happened with your prediction and explain why.

If your prediction turns out to be wrong, don't rub it out: getting it wrong and understanding why is the best way to learn.

Each activity has a link that opens the simulation already set up. If you have the worksheet on paper, go to **simulaciencia.es/en/simulations/ajuste-reaccion** and set it up as described under “Set up the simulation”.

**Remember.** Each ball is an atom and each group of joined balls is a molecule. The small number in a formula (the subscript) tells you how many atoms there are in the molecule; the big number in front (the coefficient) tells you how many molecules there are.
