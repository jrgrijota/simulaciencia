## What you will learn

With the Archimedes’ principle simulation you will find out which forces act on a body in a liquid, what the upthrust depends on and why some bodies float and others sink.

**How to work.** Each activity has three steps:

1. **Predict.** Before looking at the simulation, write down what you think will happen and why.
2. **Observe.** Set up the simulation, watch what happens and note down the data.
3. **Explain.** Compare what happened with your prediction and explain why.

If your prediction turns out to be wrong, don't rub it out: getting it wrong and understanding why is the best way to learn.

Each activity has a link that opens the simulation already set up. If you have the worksheet on paper, go to **simulaciencia.es/en/simulations/arquimedes** and set it up as described under “Set up the simulation”.

**Data.** The simulation uses g = 9.8 N/kg. Volume is in litres (L), mass in kilograms (kg), density in kg/L and forces in newtons (N). The upthrust is calculated with **E = V<sub>submerged</sub> · ρ<sub>liquid</sub> · g** and the weight with **P = m · g**.
