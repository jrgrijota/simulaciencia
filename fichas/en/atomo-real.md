## What you will learn

With the atom to scale simulation you will travel from the whole atom down to its nucleus, magnifying the image 10 times at each jump. You will find out how tiny the nucleus is, how these sizes are written in scientific notation and which particles make up each atom.

**How to work.** Each activity has three steps:

1. **Predict.** Before looking at the simulation, write down what you think will happen and why.
2. **Observe.** Set up the simulation, watch what happens and note down the data.
3. **Explain.** Compare what happened with your prediction and explain why.

If your prediction turns out to be wrong, don't rub it out: getting it wrong and understanding why is the best way to learn.

Each activity has a link that opens the simulation already set up. If you have the worksheet on paper, go to **simulaciencia.es/en/simulations/atomo-real** and set it up as described under “Set up the simulation”.

**Very small units.** 1 picometre (pm) = 10⁻¹² m. 1 femtometre (fm) = 10⁻¹⁵ m. One picometre is 1000 femtometres.
