## What you will learn

With the free fall and projectile motion simulation you will drop and throw objects on Earth, with and without air, and on the Moon. You will find out whether heavy objects fall faster than light ones, what happens to the velocity and the acceleration at the highest point and why a ball launched horizontally and another one dropped at the same time reach the ground together.

**How to work.** Each activity has three steps:

1. **Predict.** Before looking at the simulation, write down what you think will happen and why.
2. **Observe.** Set up the simulation, watch what happens and note down the data.
3. **Explain.** Compare what happened with your prediction and explain why.

If your prediction turns out to be wrong, don't rub it out: getting it wrong and understanding why is the best way to learn.

Each activity has a link that opens the simulation already set up. If you have the worksheet on paper, go to **simulaciencia.es/en/simulations/caida-libre** and set it up as described under “Set up the simulation”.

**How to read the data.** Press “Launch” to start. The object leaves a trail of dots, one every 0.2 s: if the dots get further apart, it is going faster and faster. The blue arrow is the velocity and the orange one, the acceleration. The “Motion data” drop-down gives the time, position and velocity and, at the end, the launch results. With “×0.25” everything goes four times slower, and with the − and + buttons under “Instant” you can move forwards or backwards one dot at a time.
