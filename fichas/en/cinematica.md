## What you will learn

With the Motion Graphs simulation you will watch a car or a person move along a straight track and, at the same time, see their position–time (x‑t), velocity–time (v‑t) and acceleration–time (a‑t) graphs being drawn. You will learn to read a motion graph, to build one and to tell constant-velocity motion from constant-acceleration motion.

**How to work.** Each activity has three steps:

1. **Predict.** Before looking at the simulation, write down what you think will happen and why.
2. **Observe.** Set up the simulation, watch what happens and note down the data.
3. **Explain.** Compare what happened with your prediction and explain why.

If your prediction turns out to be wrong, don't rub it out: getting it wrong and understanding why is the best way to learn.

Each activity has a link that opens the simulation already set up. If you have the worksheet on paper, go to **simulaciencia.es/en/simulations/cinematica** and set it up as described under “Set up the simulation”.

**How to read the data.** The track is a ruler from −20 to 100 m; the positive direction is to the right (“x +”). The three graphs share the time axis and a dashed cursor: the point on each graph belongs to the object at that instant. The “Time” slider moves the cursor to any moment. The drop-down “Data at the cursor time” gives the time (t, in s), the position (x, in m), the velocity (v, in m/s), the acceleration (a, in m/s²), the distance travelled and the displacement.

## Apply what you have learnt

Solve without the simulation. Write the formula, the calculation with its units and the result.

1. A cyclist passes x = 5 m at t = 0 and x = 35 m at t = 10 s, at constant velocity. Calculate their velocity, write their position equation and say where they will be at t = 20 s.
2. A car travels at 72 km/h along a straight road. How long does it take to cover 500 m?
3. An object goes from x = 0 to x = 20 m between t = 0 and t = 10 s (stage A), stays at x = 20 m until t = 15 s (stage B) and comes back to x = 0 between t = 15 and t = 20 s (stage C). Calculate the velocity in each stage and draw its v‑t graph.
4. (ESO 4) A car at 20 m/s brakes with constant acceleration and stops in 4 s. Calculate the acceleration and the distance it travels before stopping.
