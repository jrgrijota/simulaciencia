## What you will learn

With the chemical bonding simulation you will join atoms one by one: you will see how they give, gain or share electrons to form ions, molecules and lattices. Then you will test the substances they form with experiments using electric current, knocks and temperature, and you will link their properties to their structure.

**How to work.** Each activity has three steps:

1. **Predict.** Before looking at the simulation, write down what you think will happen and why.
2. **Observe.** Set up the simulation, watch what happens and note down the data.
3. **Explain.** Compare what happened with your prediction and explain why.

If your prediction turns out to be wrong, don't rub it out: getting it wrong and understanding why is the best way to learn.

Each activity has a link that opens the simulation already set up. If you have the worksheet on paper, go to **simulaciencia.es/en/simulations/enlaces-quimicos** and set it up as described under “Set up the simulation”.

**How to read the data.** Under the drawing there are three slots, “Atom A”, “Atom B” and “Atom C”. Each one shows the charge of the atom and how many electrons it has in its outer shell, for example “Valence e⁻: 7 / 8”. An atom is stable when it reaches 8 electrons in its outer shell (octet), or 2 if it is hydrogen (duet).
