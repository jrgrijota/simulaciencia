## What you will learn

With the gases simulation you will see a gas for what it is: lots of molecules that never stop moving and keep hitting the walls. You will find out what pressure is, how the pressure and volume of a gas change when you heat it, compress it or add more gas, and why a balloon can burst.

**How to work.** Each activity has three steps:

1. **Predict.** Before looking at the simulation, write down what you think will happen and why.
2. **Observe.** Set up the simulation, watch what happens and note down the data.
3. **Explain.** Compare what happened with your prediction and explain why.

If your prediction turns out to be wrong, don't rub it out: getting it wrong and understanding why is the best way to learn.

Each activity has a link that opens the simulation already set up. If you have the worksheet on paper, go to **simulaciencia.es/en/simulations/gases** and set it up as described under “Set up the simulation”. You can change the values with the controls or by typing them in.

**How to read the data.** Pressure (P) is in atmospheres (atm), volume (V) in litres (L) and temperature (T) in kelvin (K). The pressure is the average of the collisions in the last 3 seconds and, in the balloon, the rubber trembles a little because there are only a few molecules: **after each change, wait a few seconds until the pen marks the point on the graph, and then write down the values**.
