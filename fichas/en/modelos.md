## What you will learn

With the atomic models simulation you will repeat the experiment in which Rutherford discovered the nucleus of the atom. You will fire α particles at atoms and see why an experiment can force scientists to change a model.

**How to work.** Each activity has three steps:

1. **Predict.** Before looking at the simulation, write down what you think will happen and why.
2. **Observe.** Set up the simulation, watch what happens and note down the data.
3. **Explain.** Compare what happened with your prediction and explain why.

If your prediction turns out to be wrong, don't rub it out: getting it wrong and understanding why is the best way to learn.

Each activity has a link that opens the simulation already set up. If you have the worksheet on paper, go to **simulaciencia.es/en/simulations/modelos** and set it up as described under “Set up the simulation”.

**α particles** have two protons and two neutrons: their charge is positive. With the “One at a time (click)” firing mode, each click in the blue strip next to the source fires an α particle at that height. The “Angle graph” counts how much each particle is deflected, from 0° (it carries straight on) to 180° (it comes back).
