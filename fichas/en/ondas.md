## What you will learn

With the waves simulation you will make a string and a spring vibrate. You will discover that a wave carries energy but not matter, you will tell transverse waves from longitudinal ones and you will measure the quantities that describe a wave: amplitude, wavelength, period, frequency and speed.

**How to work.** Each activity has three steps:

1. **Predict.** Before looking at the simulation, write down what you think will happen and why.
2. **Observe.** Set up the simulation, watch what happens and note down the data.
3. **Explain.** Compare what happened with your prediction and explain why.

If your prediction turns out to be wrong, don't rub it out: getting it wrong and understanding why is the best way to learn.

Each activity has a link that opens the simulation already set up. If you have the worksheet on paper, go to **simulaciencia.es/en/simulations/ondas** and set it up as described under “Set up the simulation”.

**How to read the data.** On the left is the source, which produces the wave. If you click on a particle of the medium, it is marked in red as P. The “Wave quantities” box gives the amplitude (A, in m), the wavelength (λ, in m), the period (T, in s), the frequency (f, in Hz) and the speed (v, in m/s). With “×0.25” everything runs four times slower and is easier to watch.
