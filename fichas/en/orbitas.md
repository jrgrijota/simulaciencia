## What you will learn

With the orbits simulation you will repeat Newton's thought experiment: firing a cannonball from the top of a mountain faster and faster. You will find out why the Moon and satellites do not fall down, why distant planets take so long to go round the Sun and how two stars that attract each other move.

**How to work.** Each activity has three steps:

1. **Predict.** Before looking at the simulation, write down what you think will happen and why.
2. **Observe.** Set up the simulation, watch what happens and note down the data.
3. **Explain.** Compare what happened with your prediction and explain why.

If your prediction turns out to be wrong, don't rub it out: getting it wrong and understanding why is the best way to learn.

Each activity has a link that opens the simulation already set up. If you have the worksheet on paper, go to **simulaciencia.es/en/simulations/orbitas** and set it up as described under “Set up the simulation”.

**How to read the data.** In “Newton's Cannonball”, the “Launch Speed” slider sets the speed of the shot in km/s, and the “Orbital Status” panel gives the speed, the altitude above the ground, the type of trajectory and the period (how long it takes to go round once). The “2×” and “4×” buttons speed up time.
