## What you will learn

With the rate of reaction simulation you will see a chemical reaction as collisions between molecules. You will find out what it takes for a collision to produce a reaction and why temperature, concentration and catalysts make a reaction go faster.

**How to work.** Each activity has three steps:

1. **Predict.** Before looking at the simulation, write down what you think will happen and why.
2. **Observe.** Set up the simulation, watch what happens and note down the data.
3. **Explain.** Compare what happened with your prediction and explain why.

If your prediction turns out to be wrong, don't rub it out: getting it wrong and understanding why is the best way to learn.

Each activity has a link that opens the simulation already set up. If you have the worksheet on paper, go to **simulaciencia.es/en/simulations/velocidad-reaccion** and set it up as described under “Set up the simulation”.

**The reaction.** Molecules A (blue) and B (orange) are the reactants and they form the product C (green). K (violet) is a catalyst. Each molecule A and B has a light-coloured end: its **reactive site**. The simulation is random, so your numbers will not come out the same as your classmates’: look at what is bigger and what is smaller.
