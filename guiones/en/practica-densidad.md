## Aim

To measure the mass and volume of an unknown sample, calculate its density and identify what material it is made of.

## Background

The **density** (ρ) of a material is the mass of each unit of volume. It is calculated by dividing the mass by the volume:

**ρ = m / V**

Each material has its own density, so measuring it tells you what an object is made of. Here the mass is measured in grams (g) and the volume in cubic centimetres (cm³), so the density comes out in g/cm³.

The mass is measured with a balance. As the sample has an irregular shape, its volume cannot be worked out with a ruler: it is measured **by displacement**. You pour water into a measuring cylinder and read its volume; then you put the sample in and read it again. The rise in the water level is the volume of the sample:

**V = V<sub>f</sub> − V<sub>i</sub>**

Remember that 1 mL = 1 cm³.

## Method

1. Choose your sample number and the measuring cylinder in the panel.
2. Switch the balance on with its **ON** button and, with the pan empty, press **TARE** to zero it.
3. Drag the sample onto the balance pan. Write the mass in the results table.
4. Put the sample back on its tray. Bring the wash bottle over the mouth of the cylinder and add water.
5. Take the magnifier to the water and read the initial volume (V<sub>i</sub>). Keep your eyes level with the water (the yellow line on the magnifier) and read the **bottom of the meniscus**. Write it down.
6. Predict how far the water will rise. Then lower the sample **carefully** into the cylinder down to the water and let go of it there. If you drop it from the top, it will splash and you will have to start again.
7. Read the final volume (V<sub>f</sub>) with the magnifier and write it down.
8. Take the sample out and put it on its tray. Empty the cylinder with **EMPTY**, fill it again with **a different amount of water** and repeat the volume measurements. Take three measurements in total. Do not weigh the sample again: it is wet now.
