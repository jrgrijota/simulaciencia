## Aim

To find out how the pressure of a fixed amount of air changes when its temperature changes, without changing its volume, and to estimate the temperature at which the pressure would be zero.

## Background

A trapped gas pushes on the walls of its container: that is its **pressure** (P). Here it is measured in kilopascals (kPa).

If we trap air in a rigid container, its volume cannot change. When it is heated, its pressure rises; when it is cooled, its pressure falls. **Gay-Lussac’s law** (the pressure law) says that, at constant volume, pressure is proportional to absolute temperature:

**P / T = constant**

The temperature T must be in **kelvin** (K). To convert from degrees Celsius (t, in °C) to kelvin, add 273:

**T = t + 273**

To test it, you trap air in a flask, put the flask in a water bath, heat the water and read the temperature and the pressure. If the law holds, P / T should come out almost the same in every measurement.

## Method

1. Choose your set-up number in the panel.
2. With the valve **open** and the lab jack **down**, take the magnifier to the thermometer and read the room temperature. Put the yellow eye line at the end of the red column. Also read the pressure gauge. Write both in measurement 1.
3. Close the valve (lever pointing down). Do not open it again from now on.
4. Raise the lab jack all the way **up** so that the ice water covers the flask. Wait for the thermometer and the gauge to stop and write down measurement 2.
5. Switch on the hot plate and the **stirrer**. When the water is a few degrees below the temperature you want, switch the hot plate off. Wait for the thermometer and the gauge to stop, and write down the measurement.
6. Repeat step 5 until you have measurements near 20, 40, 60 and 80 °C. The hot plate switches itself off at 90 °C.
